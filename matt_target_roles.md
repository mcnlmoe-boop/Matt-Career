## Uncomfortable truths first

**1. The resume tells a "stepped back" story you haven't addressed.**
Global Product Manager ($100M P&L, 2022–24) → Senior Principal Engineer (2024–now).
A Director of Product hiring manager reads that as *left the P&L chair after two years*.
**[Likely]** it's the first question in every Track 1 screen. Decide the one-line answer
now (reorg? chose technical depth? role eliminated?) — and if you *chose* to go back to
IC work, that's evidence Track 1 isn't actually what you want.

**2. One resume cannot serve three tracks.** The current summary tries to be
product leader *and* technical authority *and* utility advisor. Each track's screener
skims for a different identity. You need three versions (different summary, different
bullet order, different competencies line), not one.

**3. "Lowest burnout" and "Director" don't go together in Track 2.**
**[Likely]** Director of Supplier Quality is one of the most firefighting-heavy roles in
manufacturing: escapes, customer complaints, supplier audits, travel. *Principal*
Component Engineer is the genuinely low-burnout title. Pick which you're optimizing for.

**4. Age signal.** "30+ years," a 1993 degree, and a 1993 maintenance-engineer job all
on page one. **[Likely]** this invites age screening at the Director level. Cut to ~20
years of detail; collapse pre-2005 into one "Earlier career" line; drop the graduation
year.

**5. The non-compete.** You're at Littelfuse; the obvious Track 1 and Track 3 employers
(TE, Omron, Eaton, Sensata, Siemens, Schneider, ABB, Carling) are competitors.
**[Guessing]** you signed something on the 2018 Hartland acquisition or a later role
change. Read it before you apply, not after an offer.

**6. Location.** Rock Falls, IL. Director roles in this space cluster in Chicago suburbs,
Milwaukee, and OEM HQs elsewhere. Are you open to relocation, a hybrid commute, or
remote-only? That one answer changes the target list more than any title choice.

## Fit by track (ranked)

### #1 — Track 3, but only *Director of Application Engineering / OEM Technical Strategy*

**Why it ranks first [Likely]:** It's the one title where *both* halves of your career
count — 25+ years of switching/magnetics depth **and** two years of P&L/VoC/OEM account
work. Supplier-side app-engineering directors are hired externally, and your patents +
curriculum authorship read as "can teach customers and sales."

**Drop or demote from this track:**
- **Senior Technical Fellow** — **[Likely]** almost always an internal promotion; rarely
  posted externally. Keep it as a long-term goal inside whoever hires you, not a search term.
- **Principal Solutions Architect** — **[Likely]** on job boards this title is
  overwhelmingly software/cloud. You'll drown in irrelevant results and screeners will
  read you as mis-targeted. Use "Principal Application Engineer" or "Technical Account
  Director" instead.

**Gap:** "Grid Modernization & Substation Support" is in your competencies with no bullet
behind it. Your utility evidence is the 2005–12 remote disconnect switch and the
2012–14 nuclear relay audit — real, but dated. Either add a recent concrete example or
soften the claim. Same for the ATS bullet ending "directly applicable to grid
reliability and backup power engineering" — that's tailoring prose, not an
accomplishment; delete it.

### #2 — Track 2, aimed at *buyer-side OEMs* where relays/contactors/transformers matter

**Why it ranks here [Likely]:** Your blind-spot-turned-asset: you've spent 30 years on
the *supplier* side, so you know where suppliers cut corners on contact materials, coil
margins, and electrical steel. That's exactly what an OEM component engineering lead is

**Target OEMs [Likely]:** HVAC (Carrier, Trane, Lennox, JCI, Daikin), generators & ATS
(Generac, Rehlko/Kohler), EV charging, data-center power (Vertiv, Eaton's DC segment),
switchgear, appliance.

**Gap — the language is wrong.** Component engineering postings screen for buyer-side
terms your resume barely uses: AVL/approved-parts management, PPAP/APQP, second-sourcing,
obsolescence/lifecycle management, supplier audits, ISO 9001/IATF 16949, cost
tear-downs. Your only explicit supplier-qualification bullet is from 1998–2000. The
electrical-steel analysis (2024–) and the 20% contactor cost-down are your best
recent evidence — rewrite them in qualification/should-cost language.

**Breadth risk:** OEM component engineering often spans semiconductors, passives,
connectors. Your depth is narrow. Target roles where electromechanical/magnetics is the
critical commodity, or say up front you're the electromechanical/power-component lead.

### #3 — Track 1, viable only in industrial/electrical product companies
