# Target Niches: Entry-Level Marketing (Lauren Moeller)

## The blind spot first

Your resume summary says you're "seeking growth, performance, lifecycle, or marketing
analytics roles." Your bullets under BPD Healthcare don't back that up — they describe
RFP coordination, pitch-deck logistics, Salesforce hygiene, and a weekly reporting
digest. That's **new-business / agency operations support**, not campaign execution.
There's no bullet showing you built an audience segment, ran an A/B test, wrote ad
copy that shipped, or moved a funnel metric. The HubSpot and Google Analytics
certifications tell a hiring manager you've *studied* growth marketing, not that
you've *done* it.

**[Likely]** This gap matters more than usual right now: multiple 2026 hiring reports
(Robert Half, Studojo) note entry-level marketing postings are flat-to-declining while
specialized/senior postings grow, and that generic "marketing coordinator" titles
without a clear specialization are the first to get cut or automated. Titling yourself
"growth marketer" while your evidence reads as "proposal manager" risks getting
screened out by both ATS keyword matching and a skeptical hiring manager. Before you
apply broadly, either (a) reframe your BPD bullets around the parts that *are*
marketing-adjacent — the weekly growth/pipeline report, competitive analysis,
messaging/proofing for voice-and-tone — or (b) target roles that explicitly value
your actual strength, which is **structured project and process management under
deadline**, not funnel optimization.

That reframing question is yours to answer, not mine to guess at. The five niches
below are ranked by fit against what you can currently prove, not what you're
currently calling yourself.

---

## 1. Healthtech / Digital Health Marketing — strongest direct fit

**[Certain]** You already have one year of paid experience inside a healthcare
marketing agency (BPD Healthcare), which is domain credibility most entry-level
candidates don't have. **[Likely]** Digital health is a real growth sector — the
global digital health technologies market was projected to grow at a ~17% CAGR
through 2026, and 2026 industry coverage describes healthcare marketing shifting
toward content/education-driven, AI-assisted strategy rather than shrinking.

- **Roles to target:** Marketing Coordinator, Marketing Analyst, Content/Communications
  Associate, Client Services Associate — inside health systems, digital health
  startups, healthcare marketing agencies (BPD's competitors), or pharma/medtech
  marketing teams.
- **Why you're credible:** Regulated-industry fluency (HIPAA-adjacent proposal work),
  Salesforce experience, agency-side process discipline.
- **Gap to close:** No evidence of hands-on campaign work. Use HubSpot/GA
  certifications as leverage, but expect to be asked for a work sample — build one
  (see recommendations below).

## 2. Fintech Marketing — matches your actual degree combination

**[Certain]** Your BS is a dual Marketing & Finance major with an Accounting minor —
that combination is unusual and is exactly what fintech marketing teams screen for,
since most marketing hires can't read a P&L and most finance hires can't write copy.
**[Likely]** 2026 fintech hiring reports (Fintech Careers, Harrington Starr) describe
the market as maturing rather than booming — hiring is shifting toward roles that
support revenue, compliance, and customer operations over "experimental" marketing
functions, and entry-level fintech marketing pay clusters around $44–55K.

- **Roles to target:** Marketing Analyst, Lifecycle/CRM Coordinator, Content Marketing
  Associate at payments companies, neobanks, wealthtech, or B2B fintech SaaS.
- **Why you're credible:** Finance/accounting coursework, quant methods for marketing,
  data analysis skill listed on resume.
- **Gap to close:** Same as above — no demonstrated campaign or lifecycle-email work.
  This is the sector where your finance credibility can substitute for it in an
  interview, but not on a resume screen.

## 3. Marketing Analytics / MarTech — plays to your quant coursework, not your job history

**[Likely]** Market research analyst roles are projected to grow ~23%, and multiple
2026 sources (Robert Half, Boston Institute of Analytics) point to AI-integrated
analytics and data-driven marketing as the fastest-growing specialization inside
marketing broadly, even as generalist entry-level postings soften.

- **Roles to target:** Marketing Analyst, Insights Analyst, Junior Marketing Data
  Analyst, CRM/Reporting Analyst.
- **Why you're credible:** SPSS, Qualtrics, Python, Excel, a completed primary-research
  project (Juice Cap) with real data collection and client presentation, Quant
  Methods for Marketing coursework, Google Analytics certification.
- **Gap to close:** This is your best *skills* match on paper but weakest *experience*
  match — your actual job history is process/coordination, not analysis. This niche
  is worth pursuing specifically because the Juice Cap project is a genuine,
  defensible work sample most applicants won't have.

## 4. Sports & Athletic Brand Marketing — strongest personal-narrative fit, weaker market fit

**[Certain]** You have a real, differentiated story here: NCAA D-I team captain,
Vanderbilt Athletics marketing intern, Wahl Clipper's athlete/ambassador-adjacent
philanthropic program. That's a coherent narrative most candidates can't fake.
**[Guessing]** but reasonably confident: this sector is smaller and more
relationship/network-driven than the other four — the search results didn't surface
strong quantified growth data, and entry-level pay in this niche (~$38–54K) trails
healthtech and fintech. Treat this as a passion-fit niche, not a growth-sector bet.

- **Roles to target:** Team/league marketing coordinator, athletic apparel or gear
  brand marketing, collegiate athletics marketing, sports agency coordinator roles.
- **Why you're credible:** Direct, recent, hands-on event-marketing experience — this
  is the one line on your resume that actually shows you executing marketing
  activities, not coordinating other people's.
- **Reality check:** Fewer roles, more competition from candidates with sports-industry
  networks. Pursue opportunistically, not as your primary funnel.

## 5. B2B Professional Services / Agency Growth & RevOps — matches your actual job, honestly

**[Likely]** 2026 reports (Robert Half, JB Search Partners) list "agency services
(marketing, PR, advertising)" and "revenue operations" among the fastest-growing
specialization areas, and note demand for hybrid roles that combine marketing with
sales-process and pipeline management — which is a precise description of what you
did at BPD.

- **Roles to target:** Business Development / Growth Coordinator, Proposal Manager,
  Marketing Operations Coordinator, RevOps Analyst, Sales Enablement Associate — at
  agencies, consultancies, or B2B SaaS companies.
- **Why you're credible:** This is the only niche where your resume bullets are a
  *direct*, not aspirational, match — RFP management, Salesforce pipeline reporting,
  cross-team deadline ownership are the literal job description.
- **Trade-off:** Less prestigious-sounding than "growth marketer," and it's adjacent
  to sales rather than brand/campaign marketing — worth naming honestly if it's not
  the work you actually want to be doing in three years.

## Bottom line

**[Likely]** Apply to healthtech (#1) and B2B agency/RevOps (#5) first — those are
where your resume needs zero reframing to be credible. Use fintech (#2) and marketing
analytics (#3) as a second wave once you've built one concrete work sample (a mock
campaign brief, a lifecycle email sequence, or an expanded write-up of the Juice Cap
project with visuals) to close the "certified but not proven" gap. Treat sports
marketing (#4) as an opportunistic track you pursue through your existing network,
not a primary search channel — the data doesn't show it growing the way the other
four are.

**What I'm not certain about:** none of this is drawn from a labor-market API or BLS
microdata — it's synthesized from 2026 hiring-trend articles and vendor reports,
which have their own incentives to sound urgent. Treat the growth percentages as
directional, not precise.
